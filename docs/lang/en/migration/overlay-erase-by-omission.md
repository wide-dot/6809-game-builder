# Overlay: erase by omission, not by repaint

## The v1 idiom

In background-erase mode a sprite drawn without refresh **stays** in video
RAM. The v1 dobkeratops exploited this: the boss body and its four optical
nerves were painted once as vertical bands (`dk-alien nerves-0..4`), then
never repainted. To make a nerve disappear when its eye died, the game
painted **erasers** over it — 45 small compiled sprites, each holding the
*background* pixels (body or black) that cover one segment of the nerve —
sequenced by an animation table (`EraserImages*` in `obj.asm`), plus a
double-buffered copy object so both video pages got erased. What could not
be covered by an eraser (the thick edges, the roots — a third of the nerve
pixels) simply lingered until the end-of-boss wipe.

## Why it breaks in overlay

The overlay engine clears and redraws the whole field every frame. Painting
an eraser achieves nothing (the nerve is repainted next frame), and once the
band objects stop being drawn the nerves vanish wholesale. The v1 idiom is
not portable: it relies on video RAM being a persistent canvas.

## The v2 idiom

Invert the mechanism: **not drawing a thing IS erasing it**. The element is
cut into unit **pieces** (one PNG per v1 eraser mask, carrying the *nerve*
pixels instead of the background pixels — same cut, same animation order,
generated by `games/r-type/src/enemies/dobkeratops/tools/gen_overlay_nerves.py`).
A manager draws, every frame, all pieces not yet removed; the erase
animation walks the same sequence as the v1 eraser table and merely stops
drawing pieces. Consequences that fall out for free:

- pieces removed at any instant form a **prefix** of the piece numbering
  (the dataset is numbered in removal order), so per-element state is one
  byte (`removed` count);
- the un-erasable "halo" of v1 disappears cleanly with its nearest piece —
  the erase is *more* faithful than v1, no lingering trail;
- no double-buffer copy objects: both pages redraw from the same state;
- for the intact state, pieces are regrouped into 16-px **bands** (canvas
  columns) — measured at +8% draw cycles over one full image, against +39%
  for drawing the 60 pieces individually.

## The manager pattern

One engine object replaces the eight v1 objects (4 nerve hitboxes that
became erasers + their copies, 4 foreground eye elements). Its fake imageset
carries the shared 80×180 canvas geometry, so `BuildSprites` performs the
window tests, parity selection and address computation, then calls the
manager's draw hook with the anchor resolved — every image of the dataset
shares that anchor. See `eyemgr.asm` / `eyepieces.asm` (r-type) and the
earlier, simpler instance of the same pattern in `tailmgr.asm`; the AABB
boxes live in a **resident** table because the engine walks its collision
lists across pages (same contract as the bullet manager).

Split constraint: a direntry holds 16 KB, and a full element set (two
shifted variants of the bands plus the pieces) exceeds it. Each parity of
the bands lives in its own unit with its own copy of the hook (the D0/D1
mapping frames of the descriptor point to different pages), and the pieces
live in a third unit reached through a resident trampoline — a cross-page
`jsr` from inside the cartridge window would swap out the calling code.

## Rule of thumb

When porting v1 code that paints background patches to hide something,
look for the *thing being hidden* and give it to a manager as removable
pieces. The v1 eraser masks are the cutting guide: their union tells you
what was erasable, their sequence tells you the animation.
